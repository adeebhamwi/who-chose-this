The detail that should unsettle a privacy lawyer, and frankly, everyone.

The users were never told.

OpenAI says its “technical approach and privacy policy” prevent it from “reassociating” the anonymized images with the people who uploaded them.

The safeguard built to protect users’ identities is the very reason none of them can be warned.

04

What an agent is

A goal, the tools to pursue it, and permission to act.

You ask for dinner. The agent searches the stores, compares prices, and places the order. You handed it the internet, your address, and a way to pay.

Then one store runs out, and it orders from a seller you never approved. Who chose that?

How every incident happens

06

Thesis

When AI agents act on their own and cause real harm, the law still looks for a person who chose to break the rules – so California’s transparency mandates, federal punishment after the fact, and securities-law disclosure each close only part of the responsibility gap, and none alone is enough.

Why existing law misses it

Every regime asks the same question: who decided?

Privacy law · CCPA

Assumes a business that decides what to do with data.

Computer crime · CFAA

Assumes a “whoever” who intends to break in.

Consumer protection · FTC Act § 5

Acts after the fact, recasting the act as a practice someone chose.

An agent defeats all three, not by being clever, but by being exactly what it was built to be.

08

The privacy paradox

The better the anonymization, the less anyone must tell.

The CCPA does not require a business to reidentify information it does not keep as personal information. The rule protects privacy for a good reason. Here, it is also why no one was notified.

09

Three responses on the table

Each closes part of the gap.

California · SB 53

Closes: a reporting duty triggered by an event, not intent.

Leaves open: notice to users, a privacy regulator, survival against federal preemption.

Federal · Hawley–Murphy

Closes: agent hacking, by assigning the act to a human.

Leaves open: privacy harm, testing environments, any duty to tell. Announced Oct. 1; not yet introduced.

Securities law · § 11

Closes: specific, liability-backed disclosure, with no chooser required.

Leaves open: private companies and the people affected. It protects investors, not users.

11

What is still missing

Three fixes.

01

An agent-incident reporting duty, enforced by the FTC

Triggered by the event, not intent; notice to the people affected, or to the public where they cannot be found.

02

A fix to California’s deidentification safe harbor

Anonymization should not block notice when an agent, not the business, picked the recipient.

03

SEC guidance that agent incidents can be material

Including incidents in which the company’s own agent was the intruder.

Builds on Sen. Cantwell’s Oct. 7, 2026 framework, which already points toward incident reporting.
